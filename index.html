I build things for brains, machines, and the space between.

I’m Pranav, an 18-year-old student exploring how intelligence works—in software, in nervous systems, and in machines that sense and act in the world.

I study computer science at VIT Vellore. I grew up in Bengaluru, started out building AI systems, and now spend most of my time somewhere between code, cognition, and hardware.

What ties my work together is a preference for things you can inspect and test. In AI that meant compilers and explicit memory. In robotics it means measuring the path from a sensor event to a physical response.

I like ambitious questions, learning outside my field, and making rough ideas concrete enough to be wrong. Eventually, I want to help define a field rather than simply work inside one.

R.01Radio astronomy · Paper

Giant radio galaxies in LoTSS DR3

Two apparently unreported giant radio galaxy candidates found in LOFAR’s LoTSS DR3 survey. N02 has a quasar host at z = 0.80245 and spans 1.127–1.165 Mpc; N12 has a galaxy host at z = 0.75551 and a conservative size of 0.795–0.818 Mpc. Specialist review supports both host associations and giant classifications; publication-priority verification remains open.

R.02AI systems · Published paper

PlanCompiler

A typed compiler that turns an LLM’s plan into a valid, executable data workflow. The model assembles typed building blocks; a validator catches structural mistakes before the compiler emits Python. On a 300-task benchmark it passed 278 tasks, against 202 for GPT-4.1 and 187 for Claude Sonnet 4.6.

R.03Space systems · Scoping

Lunar ISRU economics

Reassessing the economics of lunar in-situ resource utilisation for propellant. Prior studies used different assumptions, so I’m rebuilding them on a common basis, then updating them for reusable launch.

R.04Behavioural science · Hypothesis

Pre-performance routines vs context-based reinstatement

Can a repeated ritual become a retrieval cue? This compares how a training-paired pre-performance routine might reinstate task-relevant neural context with reinstating the training context itself.
